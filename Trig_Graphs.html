Building the Trigonometric Graphs from the Unit Circle

How does the unit circle work?

A unit circle has radius 1, so the hypotenuse of the right-angled triangle is always 1. The horizontal side is the point's x-coordinate and the vertical side is its y-coordinate.

Why x = cos(θ)

  • Adjacent side = x-coordinate
  • Hypotenuse = 1
cos(θ) = adjacenthypotenuse
cos(θ) = x1 = x

So the x-coordinate of the moving point is cos(θ).

Why y = sin(θ)

  • Opposite side = y-coordinate
  • Hypotenuse = 1
sin(θ) = oppositehypotenuse
sin(θ) = y1 = y

So the y-coordinate of the moving point is sin(θ).

What about the graph of tan(θ)?

Tangent can be found from sine and cosine: tan(θ) = sin(θ) / cos(θ). On the unit circle, tan(θ) is also the gradient of the hypotenuse (the line from the origin to the point on the circle).

tan(0°) = sin(0°)cos(0°) = 01 = 0
When cos(θ) = 0, the division is undefined. This happens at 90° + 180°n, which creates the vertical asymptotes in the tangent graph.
